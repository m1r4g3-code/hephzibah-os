The standing set of strategic-reasoning tools applied by default to any business, negotiation, pricing, or client-relationship decision in this OS — not academic, each one covers a blind spot the others have. Chess gives depth of lookahead but assumes perfect information. Poker fixes that — it's how to act well when you don't know the other side's real position. BATNA fixes not knowing your own fallback before a deal conversation. The OODA loop fixes analysis-paralysis in fast-moving situations. Voss's tactical-empathy framework fixes what to actually say in the room. Red-teaming/inversion fixes blind spots in your own plan before you commit to it — the check that would have caught the free-walkthrough-video mistake before it needed catching.

None of these replace judgment. They're the checklist that makes sure a fast, reflexively-helpful answer gets pressure-tested before it goes out, especially when real money or a real relationship is on the line.
