Act well without seeing the other side's hand. Instead of assuming you know what a client or prospect actually thinks, reason in ranges of what they might mean and weight the response by likelihood, not certainty. "Does Giovanni's message mean the opportunity's gone, or is his partner really testing our system?" is a poker question, not a chess one — chess assumes the board is visible, this situation wasn't. Resolve the range with real evidence when you can find it (a job ID that proves which system is actually running) rather than picking the reading that feels most comfortable. Where chess tells you the best sequence of moves, poker tells you how confident to be in your read of the position before you commit to a move at all.
