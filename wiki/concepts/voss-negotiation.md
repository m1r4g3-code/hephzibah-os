Chris Voss's FBI-hostage-negotiation-derived framework, on the reading list, for what to actually say once the strategy above has decided what move to make. Three tools that do the most work: tactical empathy (name and understand the other side's position better than they've said it themselves — defuses defensiveness before it starts), calibrated questions ("how am I supposed to do that?" instead of a demand — makes them solve your problem for you), and labeling ("it sounds like..." / "it seems like...") to surface an unspoken emotion so it stops driving the conversation invisibly. Where the other frameworks decide the play, this one is the actual script.
