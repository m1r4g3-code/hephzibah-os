Observe, Orient, Decide, Act — then repeat, faster than the other side can. John Boyd's fighter-pilot framework, now standard in competitive strategy: whoever cycles through the loop quicker wins, even from a weaker position, because they're adjusting to reality while the other side is still analyzing the last version of it. The counterweight to [[chess-thinking]] and [[red-team-inversion]], both of which can turn into stalling if taken too far — depth of lookahead is worthless if the position has already changed by the time you act on it. Useful gut check: am I still gathering information, or am I just avoiding the decision.
