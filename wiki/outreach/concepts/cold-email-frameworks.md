# Cold Email Frameworks — REPLY Method, JMM, Berman

Three converging frameworks. All three agree on: first email is the main event (58% of replies come from it), under 100 words, CTA must be the smallest possible ask.

---

## Framework 1 — Jason Bay's REPLY Method

**58% of replies come from the first email. Treat it as the main event, not a warmup.**

Structure of every email:

**R — Research-backed Personalization**
Show the outreach was intentional. A specific signal that relates to why you're reaching out.
- Not: "I found you on LinkedIn"
- Yes: "Saw you scaled from 3 to 8 locations last quarter"
- Yes: "Noticed you're hiring 3 ops managers this month — that's usually a sign the manual processes are about to break"

**E — Empathy**
Lead with the pain or frustration they're experiencing. Show you understand from the inside.
- "Agencies your size typically spend 30+ hours/month on manual client reporting before they automate it."
- Frame their problem from their perspective. Not "you need automation" — "you're stuck doing manually what should happen automatically."

**P — Relevant Results**
Name the result that prospects like them care about. Not your skills — their outcome.
- "Helped a similar agency eliminate that work in two weeks."
- "Cut their reporting cycle from 3 days to same-day."

**L — Low-friction CTA**
The smallest possible ask. Binary yes/no or binary choice.
- "Worth a quick call?" (yes/no)
- "Should I send over the 60-second breakdown or a case study?" (choice)
- "Mind if I send a short video on how this works?" (permission-based)

**Y — Your close**
Simple. Human. Not formal.
- "Emmanuel"
- Not: "Best regards, Emmanuel Adekoya, AI Automation Specialist"

**Total: under 80 words.**

## Framework 2 — Justin Michael's JMM/COMBO

**The bee swarm effect:** Saturation of touches creates familiarity. Prospects recognize your name before they reply.

### Opening Triple Touch (within 2 minutes of each other)
1. Phone call
2. Voicemail
3. Email

Allows the prospect to choose their channel. Creates familiarity through simultaneous contact.

### Full JMM Sequence (where phone access exists)
- 21 touches total
- 11 days
- 3 clusters
- Each cluster = 1 main email + 2 flexible bumps
- Mix: 9 emails, 3 LinkedIn messages, 6 calls (3 voicemails)

### Email-Only Adaptation (for cold outreach without phone access)
3 clusters across 11-14 days:
- Each cluster = 1 substantive email + 2 follow-up contacts (LinkedIn, email, or DM)
- Cluster 1 (Days 1-3): First email + 2 touches
- Cluster 2 (Days 5-7): Second angle + 2 touches
- Cluster 3 (Days 10-14): Final value add + 2 touches

## Framework 3 — Alex Berman's 3C Structure

**Targeting precision is the bottleneck. Not the copy.**

Structure of every email:

**1. Compliment** (personalized, specific, not generic)
One line. Something genuinely specific to them that signals you looked.

**2. Case Study** (relevant to their industry and problem)
One line. Their industry. Their type of problem. A result.

**3. CTA** (binary yes/no question)
One question. "Would you be open to a 15-minute call?"

**Body rules:**
- Under 50 words total (the discipline version)
- No links in body; only in signature
- Binary yes/no CTA only
- Replace "I would love to" with "Would you be open to?"
- No self-diminishing phrases: no "sorry to bother you," no "hope this finds you well"
- **No formal unsubscribe links** — triggers spam filters. Use conversational: "just let me know if this isn't relevant"

**Subject line formula:**
- 3-4 words
- Specific enough to seem researched, vague enough to create curiosity
- Quick questions work well: "your demo request flow" at 52% open rate to CMOs
- Avoid anything that looks like a broadcast or newsletter

## Convergence Points — Rules All Three Agree On

1. **First email is the main event.** 58% of replies come from it. Not the follow-up sequence.
2. **Under 100 words.** (Berman: under 50. Bay: under 80. JMM: concise.)
3. **Binary CTA is mandatory.** Yes/no or choice — never open-ended.
4. **Personalization must be specific.** A company-specific signal, not a category observation.
5. **Empathy before solution.** Their world first. Your capabilities never.
6. **No formal language.** Sounds human, not broadcast.

## Permission-Based Opener (2024-2026 Standard)

The highest-converting CTA format across all three frameworks: ask permission to send, rather than asking for a call directly.

| CTA type | Friction level |
|---|---|
| "Can we book 30 minutes?" | High — large commitment ask |
| "Worth 15 minutes next week?" | Medium |
| "Mind if I send you the 60-second breakdown?" | Low — permission ask |
| "Should I send the case study or the short video?" | Lowest — binary permission |

The permission-based opener lets the prospect say yes to something small, which opens the channel without the psychological resistance of a direct meeting request.

## The AI Automation Cold Email Template

Confirmed effective format for agencies and business owners:

```
Subject: [specific 3-4 word observation]

Hey [Name],

[Specific gap from their website or LinkedIn — one sentence, concrete].

I build [specific automation type] for [their type of business] — using [primary tool]. [Specific result for a similar business, one line].

Usually a [realistic timeline] build. Worth a quick call?

Emmanuel
```

**Target: under 80 words. Under 50 preferred.**

Examples of the opening gap observation line:
- "No automated reporting visible — monthly client reports are probably eating 1-2 days of someone's time."
- "Your Shopify store has 300+ products but no abandoned cart flow showing in the page source."
- "Posted 3x/week on LinkedIn last month, stopped 6 weeks ago — usually means bandwidth, not intent."
- "You're hiring 3 ops managers this month. Usually means the manual workload just crossed a threshold."

The gap observation = the R (Research) + E (Empathy) layers from REPLY, compressed into one sentence.

---

## Follow-Up Rules

- Never "just checking in" — adds no value, signals you have nothing new
- Every follow-up should add one new piece of context (a relevant finding, a new case study, a change you noticed on their site)
- Maximum 4-7 total touches before marking as dead
- If they open 3+ times without replying: they're interested but not ready. One final value-add email. Then stop.
- Replies within the first 24 hours: highest quality leads. Prioritize immediately.

---

## What Gets Cold Email to Spam

- Formal unsubscribe link (looks like bulk broadcast)
- More than 1-2 links in the body
- Spam trigger words: "free," "guarantee," "limited time," "act now," "as seen on," "earn money"
- Generic first lines that could be copy-pasted to anyone
- HTML-heavy templates (plain text converts better for cold outreach)
- Sending from a domain less than 30 days old (warm up new domains for at least 30 days before cold sends)
