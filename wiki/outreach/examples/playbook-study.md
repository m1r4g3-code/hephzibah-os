# Objection Playbook

Living document. Every real objection heard on a call gets logged here. Responses are evolved based on what actually works — not what sounds good in theory.

---

## BUDGET

### "I just don't have the funds to do it."
- **Source:** Lisa (2026-05-22)
- **Frequency:** 1
- **Caller response (actual):** Gave website URL and ended call. Quality: **folded**
- **What was missed:** No pivot attempt. Caller accepted budget objection at face value without testing whether it was a real constraint or a brush-off.
- **Better response:**
  > "Totally understand — is it that the money isn't there right now, or more that you haven't seen something worth rearranging the budget for?"
- **Why it works:** Separates a cash-flow problem (real) from a value problem (fixable). If she says "money isn't there," ask what her timeline looks like and schedule a callback. If she says "I haven't seen the value," you're back in the conversation.

## TIMING / CAPACITY

### "I'm a one-man shop and I've got all the business I need. Call back in a year."
- **Source:** Ted (2026-05-22)
- **Frequency:** 1
- **Caller response (actual):** "Okay. Yeah, I will. I appreciate that." Quality: **folded**
- **What was missed:** Ted buried a buying signal in his no: *"if I were to grow, that's something I might consider."* This is an opening. Caller closed the door themselves.
- **Better response:**
  > "That makes sense — what would need to change in your business for this to make sense sooner?"
- **Why it works:** Forces Ted to articulate his own conditions for yes. If he says "I'd need more clients," you can pivot: "That's exactly what this is designed to do — bring in more clients without you doing the work." Now you're solving the problem he named, not the one you assumed.
- **Flag:** CRITICAL LET-GO. Caller self-diagnosed post-call: "I should have let him go. I shouldn't have let him go." Learn this pivot.

## SOCIAL PROOF / TRUST

### "Do you have any travel agents using this and having success?"
- **Source:** Rachel (2026-05-22)
- **Frequency:** 1
- **Caller response (actual):** Admitted no clients yet, then retreated to free package offer. Quality: **weak_pivot**
- **What was missed:** The no-clients answer killed Rachel's trust. The free package was a panic move, not a strategy. Better to reframe the question than to answer it directly.
- **Better response:**
  > "We're in the early stages — which means you'd be positioned ahead of the agents in your area who aren't doing this yet. I can show you exactly what the content looks like. What matters more to you: being first, or waiting for someone else to prove it?"
- **Why it works:** Turns the liability (no clients yet) into an advantage (early mover). "First mover" framing works especially well for local markets where being one of the only travel agents with strong social presence is a real competitive edge.

## FALSE NO-NEED ("WE'RE ALREADY DOING THAT")

### "We're already doing that."
- **Source:** Rachel (2026-05-22) — re: social media posting
- **Frequency:** 1
- **Caller response (actual):** Did not challenge the claim. Quality: **folded**
- **What was missed:** Post-call research confirmed Rachel was NOT posting frequently. Caller had the ammunition to challenge this live but didn't use it.
- **Better response (if you've seen their page before the call):**
  > "I actually pulled up your page before calling — your last post was [X weeks ago]. I'm not saying that to be critical, I'm saying it because most travel agents I talk to are in the same position. Being 'kind of active' and being consistently visible to buyers are very different things."
- **Why it works:** Specific evidence > general claim. You can't argue with a screenshot. Kills the bluff without being aggressive — you're observing, not attacking.
- **Better response (if you haven't seen their page):**
  > "That's good to hear — what's your posting frequency looking like? I ask because the agents seeing real results are usually hitting 4-5 posts a week minimum."
- **Why it works:** Makes them self-report. If they say "a few times a month," you can pivot: "That's where the gap usually is — the algorithm treats 'sometimes' and 'never' the same way."

## TIMING / GATEKEEPER

### "[Owner] is out / at lunch / unavailable."
- **Source:** Dr. Trip Travel (2026-05-22), Kay's office (2026-05-22)
- **Frequency:** 2
- **Caller response (actual):** Accepted block; collected email at Kay's. Quality: **weak_pivot** (email is better than nothing)
- **Better response:**
  > "No problem — when's the best time to reach them directly? I'd rather call back than send an email that gets buried."
- **Why it works:** Secures a callback window, keeps you in control of timing. An email collected with no follow-up call is a dead lead.
