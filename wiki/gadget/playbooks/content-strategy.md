# Content Strategy — Angles Per Product Type

## The Principle

**Content sells the vendor. Listings sell the device.**

Most gadget pages post nothing but stock. That trains the audience to scroll past, because a feed of listings is an advertisement and everyone has learned to ignore advertisements. The follower count grows and the conversion does not.

Hephzibah content does one thing: **make the audience believe this person knows devices better than they do.** Someone who believes that will come to Hephzibah when they are ready to buy — and will not negotiate as hard, because they are buying judgement, not just hardware.

**Ratio: 70% knowledge, 20% proof, 10% listings.** The listings convert an audience the knowledge already built. Inverting the ratio is why most gadget pages plateau.

---

## The Five Angles

Every piece of content is one of these. If it is not, it is filler.

### 1. The Buying Decision
*"Which one should you actually buy?"*

The highest-value angle. Someone about to spend ₦400k+ genuinely wants this and there is very little honest version of it available in the Nigerian market.

> "iPhone 13 vs 14 in 2026: the 14 is ₦180k more for a front camera you won't notice and a battery difference of about 40 minutes. Buy the 13. Spend the difference on 256GB."

**Why it works:** it recommends against the more expensive option. Nobody selling phones does that, so doing it is instantly credible — and the credibility transfers to every other thing said afterwards.

**Formats:** X thread · IG carousel · 60s video

---

### 2. The Buyer Protection Post
*"Here's how you get scammed, and how to check."*

The trust-building engine. Directly reinforces the brand position and it is genuinely useful whether or not the reader ever buys.

> "Four ways to check if AirPods are fake before you pay: 1) Serial on Apple's checker — takes 20 seconds. 2) The case hinge — fakes have visible play. 3) Pop-up pairing animation. 4) Weight — real Pro 2 case is 50.8g. A kitchen scale settles it."

**Why it works:** giving away the verification method is the strongest possible signal that you are not the one doing the cheating.

**Formats:** X thread · IG carousel · short video with the actual check on camera

---

### 3. The Teardown
*"What 'UK used Grade A' actually means."*

Deconstruct the vague industry language. The whole business model is built on this ambiguity being closed, so every post here compounds directly.

> "Grade A doesn't mean anything. There's no body issuing it. When I say Grade A: battery 88%+, screen with no shadows or dead pixels, no more than two marks under 2mm, and every port and button tested. Here's what each of those looks like."

**Formats:** IG carousel · X thread · video

---

### 4. The Proof Post
*"Here's the inspection actually happening."*

Not a listing. The process. Battery health screen, IMEI check, ports being tested, the flaw photo being taken.

> "Every unit gets this before it's listed. Battery screen, IMEI check, all lenses, every button, speaker and mic. Takes 15 minutes. This one's at 91% and has a nick on the frame — that's going in the listing and it's ₦15k off."

**Why it works:** it makes the premium legible. A buyer who has seen the inspection understands what the price difference buys.

**Formats:** short video (best) · IG story · X photo thread

---

### 5. The Result
*"Delivered. Here's what they got."*

Social proof without begging for it. The device, the buyer's reaction, the delivery. Never a screenshot of a testimonial nobody can verify — a real delivery, dated.

**Formats:** IG story · WhatsApp status · short video

## Angle Selection by Category

| Category | Primary angle | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Used premium phones** | Buying Decision + Teardown | Highest ticket, highest confusion, most valuable advice |
| **New sealed phones** | Buying Decision | Price is public — advice is the only differentiator |
| **Premium audio** | Buyer Protection | Counterfeit fear dominates the category entirely |
| **Power & charging** | Buyer Protection | Fake chargers destroy devices — genuine stakes, genuinely useful |
| **Wearables** | Buying Decision + Teardown | Battery health decides the whole valuation |
| **Laptops** | Teardown + Proof | Highest ticket, deepest fear, most to verify |
| **Accessories** | *No standalone content* | Attach at point of sale. Never a content subject. |

---

## Cadence

| Channel | Frequency | Mix |
|---|---|---|
| **X (Twitter)** | 4–5/week | Knowledge-first. The growth engine. |
| **Instagram** | 3–4/week | Carousels and Reels. Visual proof. |
| **WhatsApp Status** | Daily | Stock, proof shots, quick answers. Warmest audience — closest to buying. |

**Consistency beats brilliance.** Three good posts a week for six months builds a following that a viral post never does. The audience that compounds is the one that expects you.

## Rules

1. **Never post a listing without knowledge in the same week.** A feed of nothing but stock is an advertisement.
2. **Every claim in content is verifiable.** Content credibility and listing credibility are one account, and it can only be drawn down once.
3. **Recommend against a sale when it is the right advice.** "Don't buy the 15 Pro yet, wait for the price to settle" costs one sale and buys the audience's belief for a year.
4. **Answer the question that gets asked three times.** Repeated DMs are free content research — the question people keep asking is the post that has not been written.
5. **Show the process, not the polish.** The inspection is more interesting than the finished listing.
6. **No trend-chasing.** A gadget page doing a dance trend loses more credibility than the reach is worth.
