# Listing Framework — How a Hephzibah Listing Is Built

A listing has one job: **make the buyer feel they know more about this device than they would holding it in a shop.** Everything below serves that.

The competing listing says "iPhone 13, UK used, neat, ₦420k, DM." A buyer reading it has to take a risk. A buyer reading a Hephzibah listing has already had the risk removed, which is what they are actually paying the premium for.

---

## Structure

```
1. TITLE          — model, storage, condition, one differentiator
2. THE HOOK       — one line on why this specific unit
3. VERIFIED SPECS — the table. The trust engine.
4. THE FLAWS      — named, located, priced
5. WHAT YOU GET   — box contents, warranty, delivery
6. THE PRICE      — stated. Never "DM for price."
7. THE CLOSE      — one question answerable in five seconds
```

---

## 1. Title

`[Model] [Storage] — [Condition], [Differentiator]`

> ✅ iPhone 13 128GB — UK Used Grade A, Battery 91%
> ✅ AirPods Pro 2 — Sealed, Verified Genuine
> ✅ MacBook Air M2 8/256 — 34 Cycles, Full Diagnostic Included

> ❌ 🔥🔥 CLEAN iPhone 13 AVAILABLE 🔥🔥
> ❌ iPhone 13 for sale
> ❌ Best iPhone 13 deal in Lagos!!!

The differentiator is the number that ends the argument before it starts. Battery percentage on a used phone. Cycle count on a laptop. "Verified genuine" on audio. Pick the one that answers the buyer's actual fear for that category.

---

## 2. The Hook — One Line

Not a sales line. A **buying-advice** line, from someone who knows the devices. This is where the product knowledge from `identity/brand.md` earns its place.

> "The 13 is the one to buy right now — same camera stack as the 14 for most use, ₦180k less."
> "This one came in with the box, receipt, and 91% battery. Those three together are rare on a used 13."
> "If you shoot video, go 256. 128 fills in about eight months."

**Test:** would this line be said to a friend who asked for advice? If it sounds like a shop, rewrite it.

---

## 3. Verified Specs — The Trust Engine

The table that no competitor bothers to produce. Every row is something that was actually checked.

```
Model            iPhone 13, 128GB, Midnight
Condition        UK Used — Grade A
Battery health   91%
IMEI             Clean — verified 2026-08-08
iCloud           Fully signed out and clear
Network          Factory unlocked
Screen           Original, no shadows or dead pixels
Face ID          Working
Cameras          All lenses tested — front and rear
Ports/buttons    Charging port, all buttons, speakers tested
```

**Rules:**
- Every row is verified or it says "not tested." Never guess.
- Include the IMEI check date. It is a date, which means it is falsifiable, which is why it is believed.
- Battery percentage is a number, not "good."
- If something is not applicable, remove the row. Do not write N/A — it reads as evasion.

---

## 4. The Flaws — Named, Located, Priced

**The section that sells the listing.**

> "Small nick on the bottom-left frame from a drop. Doesn't affect the screen, glass, or seal. Photo 4. Priced ₦15k under a clean unit because of it."

Why this works: a listing with no flaws is a listing nobody inspected. Naming the flaw proves the inspection happened, which makes every *other* claim in the specs table credible. It also removes the single most common failure mode in this business — the buyer finding something at handover and either walking away or negotiating from a position of justified suspicion.

**Rules:**
- Every visible mark gets named and located.
- Every flaw gets its own close-up photo, referenced by number.
- If a flaw affected the price, say by how much. This converts a negative into evidence of fair pricing.
- Genuinely nothing found? Write "No cosmetic flaws found on inspection 2026-08-08." Dated and specific. Never "perfect" or "like new."

---

## 5. What You Get

```
In the box       Phone, original box, USB-C cable, case (fitted)
Not included     Original charging brick, earphones
Warranty         14 days — covers any fault not caused by damage or water
Delivery         Same-day within Lagos · GIG nationwide, 1–3 days
Inspection       Lagos hand delivery — inspect fully before you pay
```

**Say what is NOT included.** It costs nothing and prevents the most common post-sale dispute. A buyer who discovers a missing charger after paying feels cheated even if the price was fair.

---

## 6. The Price

**Stated. Always.**

"DM for price" is friction that filters for the wrong buyer. A serious buyer comparing three listings skips the one that makes them work. And a price in the open is itself a trust signal — it says the number does not change based on how wealthy the buyer sounds.

```
₦420,000
Case and 20W charger included if you take it today.
```

Bundle beats discount — see `identity/pricing.md`. The listed price already contains 5–8% of negotiation room, so it can be conceded once, deliberately, without breaking the floor.

---

## 7. The Close

One question, answerable in five seconds. Never "let me know if you have questions."

> "Do you need it today or is Thursday fine?"
> "128 or 256 — how much video do you shoot?"
> "Lagos delivery or courier?"

A question that is easy to answer starts a conversation. An open-ended question is work, and work does not get done by someone browsing a timeline.

## Photography Standard

Non-negotiable. The photos carry the specs table's credibility.

| Shot | Purpose |
|---|---|
| 1. Front, on, home screen visible | Proves it powers on. Screen quality visible. |
| 2. Back, full | Body condition. |
| 3. Battery health screen (settings, unedited) | **The proof shot.** This one does the selling. |
| 4+. Every named flaw, close-up | Proves the inspection. |
| 5. Ports and frame, angled | Wear points buyers check for. |
| 6. Box and included accessories, laid out | Completeness. |

Matte black or plain background. Single light source. No filters, ever — a filtered photo of a used device reads as a hidden flaw. The flaw photos are the most important ones in the set, which is the exact opposite of how everyone else shoots.

## Channel Adaptations

Same content, different compression. Never a different set of facts.

| Channel | Format |
|---|---|
| **WhatsApp Status** | Photos 1–3. Title, battery %, one flaw line, price, close. Under 40 words. |
| **WhatsApp Broadcast** | Full spec table as text. Photos in sequence. |
| **Instagram** | Photo carousel in the order above. Caption: hook → specs → flaws → price → close. |
| **X (Twitter)** | Hook + photo 3 (battery screen). Specs in the reply thread. The battery screenshot is the scroll-stopper. |
| **Jiji** | Everything. Buyers there are comparison-shopping — completeness is the differentiator. |

## Pre-Publish Checklist

Blocks publishing. Run every time.

- [ ] Every spec row verified, or marked "not tested"
- [ ] IMEI checked and the check date is in the listing
- [ ] Battery health is a number
- [ ] Every flaw named, located, and photographed
- [ ] Missing box contents stated
- [ ] Price stated, not "DM"
- [ ] Price is at or above the floor price in the product node
- [ ] Close is one question, answerable in five seconds
- [ ] No banned phrases (`identity/brand.md`)
- [ ] Battery/diagnostic screenshot included, unedited
- [ ] Read it as a buyer with ₦420k and one shot at getting this right — is there anything you would still need to ask?

That last item is the real gate. Anything a buyer would still have to ask is something the listing should have said.
