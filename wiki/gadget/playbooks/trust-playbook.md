# Trust Playbook

Researched 2026-08-09 on Emmanuel's brief: *"customers fear buying from Ikeja vendors as they hear rumours of scam… we need to stay legit and build trust… how we can win in this problem-saturated business."*

Sources at the foot. Everything here is either a documented finding or an action derived from one.

---

## 1. The fear is specific, and that is good news

Buyers are not vaguely uneasy. They are afraid of four named things, and each has a counter.

| What buyers fear | What it actually is | The counter |
|---|---|---|
| **Cloned phones** | "China phones" — housings built to look like real models, sold as genuine. Mostly Android, but iPhone clones are documented. | IMEI + serial verified against Apple's own checker, in front of them |
| **The swap at payment** | The unit you inspected is exchanged for another **at the point of paying**. The single most-reported Computer Village trick. | Buyer holds the unit continuously from inspection to payment. Never hand it back. |
| **Stolen devices** | Local street theft plus imported stolen stock. Reported later, then blocked. | IMEI blacklist check, dated, screenshotted |
| **The seller vanishing** | No shop, no brand, no working number next week. | A named, findable, repeat-business identity |

**The finding that reframes everything:** the reporting is consistent that risk concentrates in *"roadside sellers and unregistered traders… vendors who don't have a shop or brand name to protect."*

**So the fear is not of Ikeja. The fear is of sellers with nothing to lose.**

That is the whole strategy in one line. Every trust mechanism below exists to demonstrate one thing: **I have something to lose, and you can see what it is.**

This is also why the brand carrying Emmanuel's own name is an asset rather than vanity. An anonymous page can walk away from a bad sale. A named person studying at a named university, delivering in person, cannot.

## 2. The NCC Device Management System — the biggest opening available

**October 2025: the NCC began implementing a centralised IMEI registry that blocks stolen, cloned and counterfeit phones from Nigerian networks.** When a device connects, its IMEI is checked; anything reported stolen, duplicated or unapproved gets disconnected.

Read what this actually means:

1. **Stolen phones are becoming bricks.** Not "risky" — worthless, on a delay.
2. **A wave of burned buyers is coming.** People who bought stolen stock cheaply will lose service, with no recourse against a seller who has vanished.
3. **The cheap-phone trade is losing its product.** A large share of the discount at the bottom of the Lagos market exists *because* the stock is stolen.

**For a vendor who checks IMEIs, this is the most favourable regulatory change imaginable.** It converts an invisible virtue into a visible, urgent, checkable one — and it does so on a timer.

### What to do about it

- **Check every IMEI, screenshot it, date it.** Already the rule; now it has teeth.
- **Make it the content pillar.** *"Your phone can be disconnected permanently. Here is how to check before you pay."* That post is useful whether or not the reader ever buys, which is exactly the highest-trust content there is.
- **Say it in listings, plainly:** *"IMEI checked against the stolen register on [date]. If it were flagged, the network would eventually cut it off. Yours is clean and here is the screenshot."*
- **Move before this is common knowledge.** The advantage is largest in the window between the policy existing and buyers understanding it. That window is open now.

## 3. Escrow — the one mechanic that removes the fear completely

The structural problem in every Nigerian online sale: **somebody has to go first, and whoever goes first carries all the risk.** Buyers fear paying upfront; sellers fear releasing goods unpaid. Most stalled transactions die there.

**As of 2026 there are WhatsApp-native, bank-backed escrow services in Nigeria** — EscrowPay, SentiBud, SymbolPay — several partnered with CBN-licensed banks (Access, Zenith, GTB). Money is held by a neutral party and released only when the buyer confirms.

**Why this matters more for Hephzibah than for anyone else:**

- Under the broker model there is **no capital at risk** — so escrow costs Emmanuel nothing but a day or two of settlement. A stockist has cashflow reasons to resist it. He does not.
- It converts the strongest objection — *"why should I trust you?"* — into *"you don't have to."*
- **Almost nobody offers it unprompted.** Offering it before the buyer asks is a signal no scammer would ever send, because a scammer cannot survive it.

### The line to use

> "If you'd rather not send money to someone you haven't met — use escrow. You pay in, I deliver, you confirm, then it releases to me. I'll set it up."

**Action:** pick one provider, open the account, run one live transaction to learn the flow, then put it in the listing template. Queue item.

## 4. What the global refurbished market standardised — and what to copy

Back Market and Swappa solved the same trust problem at scale. The mechanics are public and most cost nothing to adopt:

| Their mechanism | Hephzibah version |
|---|---|
| Standardised grades A–C, published definitions | **Publish the grade scale.** "Grade A" means nothing until the definition is written down and applied consistently. |
| Minimum 30-point inspection | The three-screen check plus IMEI is a start. Write the full checklist and publish it. |
| Third-party diagnostic report | The vendor grading label — already arriving on some units, currently thrown away. **Photograph it.** |
| 30-day returns, stated up front | 14 days is credible at this scale. **Written, not implied.** A short honest warranty beats a long vague one. |
| Guaranteed not stolen, locked or fraudulently sourced | Say it explicitly. It is already true. |
| Minimum 80% battery health standard | **Adopt a floor and publish it.** A stated minimum is a promise; a case-by-case number is a negotiation. |

**The pattern across all of them:** none of this is technology. It is *writing the standard down and then meeting it every time.* The moat is the consistency, not the checklist.

## 5. What separates resellers who last

From the trade literature, and it converges on the same few things:

- **"Reputation is your balance sheet."** In a market where everyone sells the same phone from the same source, the only compounding asset is whether people believe you.
- **Real photos, never stock images.** Already the rule here — and the ProBook catalogue photo we caught is exactly the failure it warns about.
- **Same-day replies.** Cheap, and most vendors fail it.
- **Accurate grading reduces returns**, and returns are what actually destroy small resellers.

### One finding that contradicts earlier advice in this OS

> **"The resellers who grow fastest are not the ones on every platform — they are the ones who dominate one platform first."**

Earlier sessions pushed channel expansion (X, Instagram) off the back of "posting isn't producing deals." **That was the wrong conclusion from the right observation.** The problem was never too few channels — both closed deals came from personal relationships, and WhatsApp is where those relationships already live.

**Corrected: master WhatsApp completely before adding anything.** Status discipline, broadcast list, same-day replies, escrow offered by default, every unit documented. Add a second channel only when WhatsApp is producing consistently.

## 6. The strategy, in one page

**Position:** *The gadget seller who has something to lose.*

Not "trusted" — every vendor claims that. **Demonstrably accountable**, which almost none can afford to be.

**The five proofs, in order of cost to copy:**

1. **Escrow offered before it is asked for.** A scammer cannot offer this. Nearly free.
2. **IMEI checked, screenshotted, dated** — against a register that now actually disconnects phones.
3. **The inspection label photographed**, not discarded.
4. **A written warranty and a published grade scale.** Standards, not adjectives.
5. **A real name, a real face, a real location, a real institution.** The opposite of the roadside trader the reporting warns about.

**The timing:** the NCC registry makes checking urgent, and the escrow rails only became WhatsApp-native recently. Both advantages are largest right now and shrink as they become normal.

**The discipline:** one channel, done completely, before any second one.
