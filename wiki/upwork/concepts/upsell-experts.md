# Upsell and Client Expansion Experts

The question: **who is the best at converting a small/low-budget client into a big fish?**

---

## The Ryan Ramshaw Model (primary)

Ryan's own model IS the answer. He doesn't teach a separate "upsell framework" — he teaches the mechanism:

1. Over-deliver on every contract. Quote under, deliver over.
2. Client gets more than they expected → 5-star review, warm relationship
3. Client comes back → new fixed-price scope → new contract
4. Each phase grows: $2k → $8k → $30k → $180k for 6 months

The $30k client that became $180k started as an invitation to a single project. 10 hours of discovery calls plus a free POC later → 6-month engagement.

**The mechanism:** Trust compounds. The first project buys you access to the real budget.

---

## Blair Enns — Win Without Pitching

**Best for:** Positioning as expert (not order-taker), the "Value Conversation," going from vendor to trusted advisor

The 12 proclamations in "Win Without Pitching Manifesto" are about reclaiming the expert position in the client relationship. The key one relevant to upselling:

*"We will specialize" → "We will have a process" → "We will have a value conversation before discussing price"*

The Value Conversation: before naming a price, have a conversation about what the outcome is worth to the client. "If we solve this problem perfectly, what does that mean for your business?" Their answer tells you the ceiling. Price below that ceiling.

**Resources:**
- Book: "Win Without Pitching Manifesto" (~$15)
- Book: "Pricing Creativity: A Guide to Profit Beyond the Billable Hour" (more tactical)
- Website: winwithoutpitching.com
- Podcast: appearances on multiple shows (search "Blair Enns value conversation")

**Apply when:** Transitioning from "how much do you charge" to "what is this worth to you"

## Jonathan Stark — Ditching Hourly

**Best for:** Value-based pricing mechanics, converting clients from hourly to fixed/value pricing, proposal structure that sells outcomes

Key formula: Max Price = (Client Desire × Budget) ÷ Available Options

In plain English: the less the client has shopped around and the stronger their need, the higher you can price. Your job is to be the only obvious choice.

The approach to expanding small clients:
1. Start with a small project
2. Add 85%+ premium over what an hourly estimate would be
3. Open a discovery conversation (not a proposal)
4. Proposals sell outcomes, offer options, quantify ROI
5. Each completed phase creates permission to propose the next

**Resources:**
- Book: "Hourly Billing Is Nuts" (free PDF at jonathanstark.com/hbin)
- Podcast: "Ditching Hourly" — episode "Converting Hourly Clients to Value Pricing" is directly applicable
- Website: jonathanstark.com
- Newsletter: daily pricing tips

**Apply when:** Client asks "how much?" before scope is defined. Before writing any SOW.

---

## Alex Hormozi — Value Ladder

**Best for:** Client ascension model, offer creation, going from low-ticket to high-ticket in a defined ladder

Hormozi's model: create a value ladder where each service level delivers more value at a higher price. Clients start at the bottom (low risk, low price) and ascend naturally as they see results.

Translated to Upwork:
- Level 1: Audit or small automation ($500-1k) — low risk, client gets a win
- Level 2: Full system build ($3-8k) — client trusts you now
- Level 3: Full retainer / ongoing partner ($5k+/month) — you're the solution

Each level leads naturally to the next. You don't pitch the retainer on day 1. You deliver the audit so well they ask you to do the full build.

**Resources:**
- Book: "$100M Offers" (most applicable)
- YouTube: Alex Hormozi channel
- Book: "$100M Leads" (for acquisition context)

## The Single Most Applicable Insight for Emmanuel Right Now

The client who starts at $500 can become a $50k client. But not if you pitch the $50k from the start.

The sequence: small win → trust → bigger ask → bigger win → they refer others → you're their only option.

Ryan's student who made $100k from ONE client? Started with a profile-level conversation. One client. Multiple phases. The client saw results, kept buying.

Don't think "what's the biggest deal I can close today." Think "what's the smallest thing I can deliver that earns the right to propose the next thing."
