# Business Model Library

Before writing any proposal or entering any discovery call, identify the client's business model and the metrics that drive their decisions. Every industry has a small set of numbers that executives check daily. Use those numbers in the proposal. A proposal that uses WIP accounting language to an engineering firm proves comprehension in one line. Generic automation language proves nothing.

---

## Engineering / Construction Services

**How the business works:**
Project-based. Revenue is earned as work is delivered, not when contracts are signed. Cash flow is tight because materials and labor are paid before the client pays invoices. The business lives and dies by how many jobs are running, how well they're staffed, and how quickly the client pays.

**Core metrics:**
- **WIP (Work In Progress):** Revenue earned but not yet billed, or billed but not yet received. The central financial metric in any engineering/construction business. If WIP is growing without corresponding cash collection, the business is at risk.
- **Utilization rate:** What percentage of billable hours are your people actually working on paying jobs vs. sitting idle or doing admin. The efficiency multiplier.
- **Project margin:** Revenue minus direct costs (labor, materials, subcontractors) per job. Where profitability is actually created or destroyed.
- **Aged receivables:** How old is the money owed to you? 30/60/90 day buckets. Cash flow visibility.
- **On-time delivery rate:** Are jobs completing on schedule? Delays eat margin and damage client relationships.

**Common pain points:**
- Reporting scales with the number of jobs running — someone compiles data manually
- Cash flow is invisible until the accountant runs a report at month-end
- Job scheduling is managed manually or in spreadsheets
- Field workers and project managers operate without consolidated visibility

**Language to use in proposals:**
"WIP by job," "utilization across your teams," "cash position against aged receivables," "job completion rate vs. scheduled delivery," "project margin vs. estimate"

## Agencies (Marketing, Design, Development, PR)

**How the business works:**
Revenue is a mix of retainers (monthly fixed fees) and project work (scoped, one-time). Profitability depends on how many billable hours are delivered vs. how many the client was charged for. Admin time (reporting, comms, account management) is the margin killer.

**Core metrics:**
- **Billable hours:** Time spent on client work that can be invoiced. The fundamental unit.
- **Realization rate:** Of the hours that were billable, what percentage actually got billed? Below 85% = margin problem.
- **Utilization rate:** Of all available hours, what percentage were billable? Below 70% = capacity problem.
- **Retainer mix:** What % of revenue is recurring (retainers) vs. one-off? Higher retainer mix = more stable, more predictable.
- **Client churn rate:** How many clients renew each year? Churn is the silent business killer.
- **Average revenue per client:** Are clients growing or shrinking?

**Common pain points:**
- Monthly client reporting consumes 1-2 days across the team
- New client onboarding is chaotic and inconsistent
- Follow-ups and approvals live in email threads
- Account managers spend more time on admin than strategy

**Language to use in proposals:**
"Monthly report automation," "retainer renewal rate," "client health tracking," "billable hour capture," "onboarding workflow"

## E-commerce

**How the business works:**
Revenue from selling products, usually with high transaction volume. Profitability depends on acquiring customers cheaply (CAC), keeping them long (LTV), and managing inventory efficiently. Margin lives in operational efficiency.

**Core metrics:**
- **GMV (Gross Merchandise Value):** Total transaction volume through the store
- **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost):** What does it cost to bring in one new customer?
- **LTV (Lifetime Value):** How much does a customer spend over their entire relationship?
- **LTV:CAC ratio:** Target is 3:1 or better. Below 1:1 = existential problem.
- **Return rate:** What % of orders come back? High returns destroy margin.
- **Inventory turnover:** How fast does stock sell? Slow-moving inventory ties up cash.
- **Abandoned cart rate:** How many people start checkout and don't finish?

**Common pain points:**
- Manual order processing and fulfillment coordination
- No post-purchase flow (review requests, upsells, win-back sequences)
- Abandoned cart emails either not set up or generic
- Customer segmentation and personalization done manually or not at all

**Language to use in proposals:**
"Abandoned cart recovery," "post-purchase sequence," "inventory reorder alerts," "LTV optimization," "customer segmentation"

## SaaS (Software as a Service)

**How the business works:**
Subscription revenue that compounds. Profitability comes from keeping customers longer than it costs to acquire them. The challenge: churn destroys compounding. Growth requires both new customer acquisition and expansion of existing accounts.

**Core metrics:**
- **MRR / ARR (Monthly / Annual Recurring Revenue):** The health metric. Everything else is commentary.
- **Churn rate:** What % of MRR is lost each month from cancellations? Above 2% monthly = serious problem.
- **NRR (Net Revenue Retention):** After churn AND expansion, are existing customers worth more or less than last year? Above 100% = the business grows even if acquisition stops.
- **CAC:** Cost to acquire one new customer.
- **Payback period:** How many months does it take to recover CAC from subscription revenue?
- **DAU/MAU:** Engagement signal. If users aren't using it, churn is coming.

**Common pain points:**
- Onboarding is manual and inconsistent (first 30 days predict retention)
- No automated churn detection (usage signals go unread)
- Support volume scales with customers but support team doesn't
- Trial-to-paid conversion is not tracked or optimized

**Language to use in proposals:**
"Trial-to-paid conversion," "onboarding automation," "churn signals," "NRR improvement," "activation flow"

## Professional Services (Law, Finance, Consulting)

**How the business works:**
Time-based or project-based billing. Revenue comes from deploying expert human hours against client problems. Profitability depends on leverage (how many clients can each senior person serve with junior support) and realization (how much of the work is actually billed).

**Core metrics:**
- **Utilization rate:** What % of available hours are billable? Below 65% = capacity underutilized.
- **Realization rate:** Of billable hours worked, what % are actually invoiced and collected?
- **Leverage ratio:** How many junior staff does each senior partner/director manage? Higher leverage = more scalable.
- **Matter management:** How many active client engagements is each person running?
- **Average matter value:** What does a typical engagement generate?

**Common pain points:**
- Document creation and template management is manual
- Client update emails and status reports are written from scratch each time
- Time tracking is manual and often incomplete (billable hours go unbilled)
- Business development (proposals, follow-ups) competes with delivery time

**Language to use in proposals:**
"Matter management," "client reporting automation," "document assembly," "time capture," "partner leverage"

## How to Use This Library

1. Identify the client's industry from their job post or profile
2. Read the relevant section before writing any proposal or call brief
3. Use their metrics in the situation paragraph of the proposal
4. Ask about their specific metric performance during the discovery call
5. Position the automation outcome in terms of how it improves the metric they care about

The goal is not to sound like you know their industry better than they do. The goal is to prove you understand what they measure and why it matters to them. One correct metric used in context proves more than three paragraphs about your automation experience.
