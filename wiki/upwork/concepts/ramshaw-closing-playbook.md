# Ramshaw Closing Playbook

Source: "I Closed a $15,000 Client On Upwork (Live)" — Ryan Ramshaw, 2026-03-02

---

## The $15k Close — What Actually Happened

This was an **inbound deal** (client sent invitation, did not apply). Ryan's profile ranked high enough that the client sought him out personally — even though 60+ other people had already applied to the same job.

### Step-by-step

1. **Invitation received** — client saw Ryan's profile and reached out
2. **Loom sent** — short, casual, under 90 seconds. "I can help, here's my portfolio, not much context in your post so message me." No long text proposal.
3. **Client replied** asking to book a call
4. **ONE scheduling link sent** — Upwork scheduler or Zoom. No "I'm available Monday/Wednesday/what's your timezone." One link, they pick a time.
5. **Discovery call** — brief rapport → "Tell me more about the project and how I can help" → shut up and listen
6. **Fathom recording** — Ryan stays fully present because Fathom captures everything
7. **Manis SOW** — paste Fathom transcript into Manis AI → "What should we charge? Generate a beautiful SOW PDF." → sends wireframes + SOW within 24 hours
8. **NDA request** — client needed email to send NDA. Ryan: "Send me a $10 contract so I can share my email." Client creates $10 milestone, email exchanged legally.
9. **Second call** — reviewed SOW, refined, closed
10. **Contract** — 40% upfront ($4,500), 30% at midpoint, 30% on completion
11. **Price** — $15k when everyone else bid $4-5k

## The $180k Pattern — How Big Deals Work

The $30k project that was actually $180k for 6 months:
- 10 hours of calls total to close (Ryan wasn't on all of them)
- Built a **free POC worth $5-10k** before seeing a contract
- Weeks of work with zero income before the deal closed
- Result: $180k for 6 months

**This is the pattern for $100k+ deals:**
Inbound invite → deep discovery (multiple calls) → free POC to remove all client risk → SOW → big contract

This is NOT the starting strategy. This is what inbound + strong profile unlocks.

## Discovery Call Frame

Ryan = the doctor. Client = patient describing symptoms.

**Call structure:**
1. Brief rapport: 30-60 seconds max (where are you from, etc.)
2. "Okay cool, so tell me more about the project and how I can help"
3. **Shut up.** Let them talk for as long as possible.
4. The more they talk, the more they invest, the more likely they hire
5. Listen for: scope signals, pain points, what to charge
6. Questions from the stack — use to understand, not to show off
7. Close: "Based on what you've told me, I think I can help. I'll put together a scope and send it over by [specific day]. Does that work?"

**Tools:**
- **Fathom** — call recording + transcript (free). Stay present, let Fathom remember.
- **Manis** — paste transcript, generate SOW PDF in minutes
- Send ONE scheduling link. Never negotiate time slots via message.

## Quote Under, Deliver Over

Tell them 12 weeks. Deliver in 8. They feel like they won.

This is the formula for:
- 5-star reviews (always)
- Repeat business (they come back)
- Referrals (they tell others)
- Zero churn (no bad contracts, no JSS damage)

"For every single client I've had in the last 90 days — guaranteed you could ask any of them, I over-delivered like crazy."

## The $10 Contract (NDA Workaround)

Upwork TOS prevents sharing contact info before a contract exists.

When client needs your email: "Just send me a $10 contract so I can share my email with you." Clean, legal, simple. Client creates a $10 milestone. You accept. Email exchanged. NDA signed off-platform. Real contract follows.

---

## What "Big Deal" Pricing Actually Means

60 people bid $4-5k. Ryan bid $15k. He got it.

Ryan's six-figure deals: $150k, $160k, $180k, $200k+. All inbound. All started with an invitation.

The pricing logic: not hourly, not phase-based, not "what's reasonable." Value-based. What is the outcome worth to this business? If the automation system allows them to manage double the businesses without more staff — the system is worth $500k+ to them. Charge a fraction of the value.

**The rule:** Close on value, not on cost. The client who resists $15k but not $4k is not your client.
