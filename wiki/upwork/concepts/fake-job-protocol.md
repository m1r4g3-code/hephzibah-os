# Fake Job Protocol

Source: Ramshaw Principle 36. "Out of 18 million freelancers, there are probably fewer than 100 serious, active people in your niche." This protocol maps that landscape in 24 hours.

---

## When to Run

- When entering a new niche or sub-niche for the first time
- When proposal reply rate drops below 10% (competitive landscape may have shifted)
- When profile or proposal has gone 30+ days without a win
- On a new account before the first proposal batch
- Quarterly maintenance: once every 90 days to detect new competitors

---

## The Protocol

### Step 1 — Create Client Account

Use a separate email. Do NOT use the main Upwork account (`~011b48d2eabbfa6361`).
The fake job is posted from a client perspective. The main freelancer account must not be connected.

Use: adekoyaafolasade29@gmail.com (personal/work email — not the Upwork account email)

### Step 2 — Write the Fake Job Post

Post a realistic job in the target niche. Requirements:
- Sound like a real client who knows what they need
- Name a specific tool, platform, or process the niche works with
- Include one specific problem that real clients in this niche face
- Set a realistic budget (under-budgeted on purpose — $500-1,500 for a job usually worth $3k+)
- Mark as "Intermediate" skill level

**AI automation / n8n niche template:**
```
Looking for an n8n expert to automate [specific workflow] for [industry type].
The system needs to handle [specific process] with [specific technical requirement].
Must be reliable — [name one failure mode they should already know about].
Timeline: [realistic but tight].
Budget: [under-market to attract all applicants].
```

### Step 3 — Invite Top Profiles

After posting, invite the top 5-7 freelancer profiles you bookmarked from research
(Principle 27: search $100k+ earners, bookmark their profiles).

This guarantees the best competitors apply, not just whoever happens to see the post.

### Step 4 — Read Every Proposal

Read all proposals that come in within 24-48 hours. Analyze:

| What to extract | Why |
|---|---|
| First lines (openers) | What patterns do competitors lead with? |
| Failure modes they name | What do they know vs what do they miss? |
| Proof structure | Do they name clients? Specific metrics? |
| Voice and register | Generic or human? AI-sounding or experienced? |
| Closing questions | Do they open loops or beg for the job? |
| Parallel constructions | Count the triplets — a proxy for AI generation |
| What nobody names | THIS is where the gap lives. The thing Tier 1 misses = the weapon. |

### Step 5 — Extract the Intelligence

Document findings to `upwork/market/patterns/proposal-intel-YYYY-MM-DD.md`.

Format:
```
Tier 1 (best 3-5): what they do well
Tier 2 (competent but generic): what's average
Tier 3 (filtered immediately): what to never do
Tier separation patterns: exact signals
Gaps in Tier 1: what even the best don't do
```

### Step 6 — Close the Fake Job

After 24-48 hours:
- Mark the job as closed / filled
- Do NOT hire anyone (this is research only)
- Consider leaving a polite note to any Tier 1 applicants who sent exceptional proposals
  (optional — some operators do this as professional courtesy)

### Step 7 — Apply the Intelligence

Immediately update:
- Proposal framework with the identified Tier 1 gaps
- Voice guide with new patterns to avoid
- Personal proposal template with the uncovered weapons

The intelligence decays within 90 days as competitors adapt.
Run the protocol quarterly to stay ahead.
