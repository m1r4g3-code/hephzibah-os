# Proposal Anatomy

The structural breakdown of a proposal that converts. This is not a template. Templates are why most AI proposals fail. This is the skeleton — what each line is doing, why it exists, what makes it work.

---

## The Four-Part Structure

```
[HOOK]       — 1 sentence. Their situation. Not "I".
[DIAGNOSIS]  — 2-3 sentences. Name the real problem.
[PROOF]      — 1-2 sentences. One specific relevant thing.
[QUESTION]   — 1 sentence. Sharp. Opens a conversation.
```

Total: 150–250 words. Not more. Not less.

---

## The Hook

**What it must do:** Stop the scroll. Make the client read the next line.

**The rule:** Start with THEIR situation, not yours. They don't care who you are yet. They care whether you understand their problem.

**Good hook patterns:**
- Start with a specific observation about their post: "Your dashboard migration has a timing problem that usually doesn't show up until week 3 of development..."
- Start with a reframe of their stated problem: "The job says 'build an integration' but what you're actually managing is a data ownership dispute between your CRM and your billing system..."
- Start with the consequence of their stated problem: "The 40-hour manual reporting process you mentioned is solvable in about a week — but the real question is whether the automaton catches the 12 edge cases your team has learned to handle manually..."

**What never works:**
- "Hi, I'm Emmanuel and I'm a full-stack developer with 3 years of experience..."
- "I came across your post and I'm very interested in this opportunity..."
- "I am a passionate developer who loves building solutions..."

---

## The Diagnosis

**What it must do:** Name the real problem — often different from the stated problem. Show business context understanding.

**The pattern:** Client says X. The real issue is Y. Y exists because of Z (business context).

**Good diagnosis examples:**

_Client posts:_ "Need a React developer to fix bugs in our dashboard."
_Weak proposal:_ "I can fix your React bugs. I have extensive React experience."
_Strong diagnosis:_ "Dashboard bug backlogs usually mean one of two things: the state management layer is fighting the data model, or the component hierarchy wasn't designed for the data volume you're now running. Either way, the fixes aren't just bug fixes — they're architectural decisions. Patching them without that context makes them come back in three weeks."

---

## The Proof

**What it must do:** Give them one reason to believe the diagnosis is coming from real experience, not confidence theater.

**The rule:** One specific thing. Not a list of skills. Not "I have experience in X, Y, Z." One concrete example that maps directly to their situation.

**Strong proof patterns:**
- "I built this exact pattern for a German medical admin team — 4 workflows in 4 days, all on n8n."
- "I solved this data sync problem for a SaaS company last month — their manual reconciliation went from 6 hours to 12 minutes."
- "The architecture you're describing has a well-known scaling bottleneck at the [X] layer — I've hit it twice and solved it both times."

**Weak proof:**
- "I have 3 years of experience with React."
- "I am proficient in the required technologies."
- "I have completed many similar projects."

---

## The Question

**What it must do:** Open a conversation. Signal senior thinking. Require the client to actually engage.

**The rule:** One question. Specific to this job. Not "when can we talk?" or "what's your timeline?" — those are generic. The question must show you've already been thinking about their problem.

**Strong questions:**
- "Are you planning to extend this dashboard to mobile? That changes the state architecture decision significantly."
- "Is the data coming from one source or multiple systems? That's the thing that determines whether this is a 2-day or 2-week integration."
- "Did your previous developer leave documentation on the API layer, or are we reverse-engineering that?"

**Weak questions:**
- "When would you like to get started?"
- "Can we schedule a call to discuss further?"
- "What is your timeline for this project?"

---

## Length Discipline

150–250 words. This is not arbitrary.

- Under 150: you skipped the diagnosis or the proof. It's too thin.
- Over 250: you're dumping information. The client scans, doesn't read.

Elite freelancers know: brevity signals confidence. A wall of text signals insecurity. It says: "I'm compensating for something."

---

## What Kills Proposals

Ranked by damage:

1. **Generic hook** — they can tell it's been sent to 50 jobs. Stop reading immediately.
2. **No diagnosis** — just skills listed + willingness to work. Nothing to differentiate.
3. **AI-smell phrases** — "I would be delighted", "as per your requirements", "leverage my expertise". Instant trust destroyer.
4. **Proof is a credential, not an outcome** — "certified in X" does not prove you can solve their problem.
5. **CTA is a plea** — "I hope to hear from you" signals low leverage. They won't.
6. **Wrong length** — too long: arrogant use of their time. Too short: didn't try.
