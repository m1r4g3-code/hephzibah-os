# Client Types Playbook

Named client archetypes with diagnosis signals, hidden fears, and proposal/conversation response strategies. Each archetype requires a different approach.

---

## Archetype 1: The Burned Founder

**Profile:** Has hired freelancers before. Got burned — disappeared mid-project, delivered wrong thing, poor communication. Now skeptical. Posts detailed requirements because they've learned vague requirements = vague results.

**Detection signals:**
- "Need someone reliable and communicative"
- "Previous freelancer didn't work out"
- "Long-term relationship if this goes well"
- Detailed job post with specific requirements
- Moderate spend history but below-average review count (some hires ghosted before leaving reviews)

**Hidden fear:** "This person will also disappear or deliver something unusable."

**What they need to believe:** "This one is different. They've thought about my problem. They won't ghost."

**Proposal strategy:**
- Acknowledge the complexity of their requirements (shows you read it)
- Name the communication structure upfront ("I send weekly updates and flag blockers same-day")
- Milestone structure: break project into clear checkpoints
- End with: "I work with milestone structures — [X] at kickoff, [Y] at mid-point, final at completion. This way you're always seeing progress before full payment."

**Avoid:** Anything that sounds like you're promising the world. Burned clients don't believe promises. They believe systems.

---

## Archetype 2: The Overwhelmed Technical Founder

**Profile:** Knows exactly what they need technically but doesn't have time to specify it properly. Building a product, wearing 5 hats, no bandwidth to handhold. Wants someone who can run with minimal direction.

**Detection signals:**
- Sparse job description despite complex requirement
- "Ideally someone who can figure out the details"
- Fast responder to proposals (they need this solved NOW)
- High hourly rate accepted in past (pays for talent)
- Young company, small team

**Hidden fear:** "This will become a second job — I'll spend more time managing them than if I'd built it myself."

**What they need to believe:** "This person will handle it. I can brief them once and get a result."

**Proposal strategy:**
- Show autonomous thinking in the proposal itself (the proposal IS the proof of concept)
- Include a clarifying question that shows you've already started thinking
- Mention: "I'll document decisions as I go so you're always caught up without needing to be in the details"
- Short proposal — they don't have time to read long ones

**Avoid:** Too many questions in the proposal. Every question signals they'll need to manage you more.

---

## Archetype 3: The Business Owner Without Technical Understanding

**Profile:** Has a business problem and has translated it (imperfectly) into a technical requirement. The job description has the right instinct but wrong framing. Often using tech terms they've heard but don't fully understand.

**Detection signals:**
- Job description mixes business goals with technical requirements incorrectly
- Budget seems low for what they're asking (they don't know the complexity)
- "I want it to work like [consumer app]" comparisons
- First or second Upwork hire
- No technical background apparent in their profile or job history

**Hidden fear:** "I don't understand this enough to know if they're doing it right."

**What they need to believe:** "This person translated my business problem into tech for me. I can trust their judgment."

**Proposal strategy:**
- Reframe their requirement in business outcome language first
- Then describe the technical solution in plain terms
- Use the challenger reframe: "What you're describing will work, but there's a simpler way to get the same outcome..."
- Be their advisor, not their vendor

**Avoid:** Technical jargon. They'll feel talked down to or lost. Both kill trust.

---

## Archetype 4: The Agency Sub-Contractor

**Profile:** An agency running a client project, needs execution help. Experienced with Upwork. Has done this many times. Wants reliability, clear deliverables, no surprises. Will hire fast if you're the right fit.

**Detection signals:**
- Job description is extremely well-specified (they've done this before)
- Posted under an agency profile or company account
- Short timeline, specific scope
- Has hired many freelancers (100+ hires in history)
- Will ask for references or portfolio

**Hidden fear:** "They'll need too much explanation or miss the deadline."

**What they need to believe:** "This person is professional, will deliver exactly what was scoped, and won't require hand-holding."

**Proposal strategy:**
- Match their professional register
- Address the specific deliverables directly (they appreciate precision)
- Mention past delivery track record, not just skills
- End with: "Happy to share similar work samples. What's your preferred format for the hand-off?"

**Avoid:** Over-selling. Agencies have seen every pitch. Just show you understand the scope and can deliver.

---

## Archetype 5: The Premium Client

**Profile:** Established business, serious budget, thoughtful about who they hire. Has a real problem with real ROI at stake. Will research you before hiring. Moves slower but pays better and treats freelancers professionally.

**Detection signals:**
- $5,000+ total Upwork spend
- 70%+ hire rate
- Average hourly paid: $75+
- Job post is strategic, not tactical ("we're building a system to..." not "we need someone to fix...")
- Professional writing in the job description

**Hidden fear:** "They won't understand the business context and we'll get a technically correct but strategically wrong result."

**What they need to believe:** "This person thinks like a business person, not just a developer."

**Proposal strategy:**
- Lead with business outcome, not technical capability
- Show you understand the market or industry context
- Include a strategic observation they hadn't thought of (the challenger reframe)
- Propose a scoping call as the next step (not immediate start)

**Avoid:** Jumping straight to implementation. Premium clients want to know you'll think before you build.

---

## Archetype 6: The Red Flag Client

**Profile:** Posts jobs that look real but have warning signs throughout. Will drain time and JSS. Must be identified and skipped early.

**Detection signals (any 2 = skip):**
- "Will pay after I review the work" (unpaid trial)
- "Budget is flexible for the right person" with zero spend history
- "I need someone who can do everything" (undefined scope)
- "ASAP" with no budget context
- Job reposted 3+ times
- Reviews from past freelancers mention "scope creep" or "didn't communicate"
- Multiple open contracts simultaneously (5+)
- Average review below 4.0

**Response:** Skip. No exceptions. No "but the project looks interesting." The patterns are clear.
